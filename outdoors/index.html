The outdoor recreation sector is substantially larger than most people assume and behaves like an industry: it has supply chains, seasonal cash flow, land-access dependencies and a regulatory environment that can change the economics of a whole region.

This desk covers it accordingly. Public lands policy, access, conservation funding and the businesses — outfitters, guides, retailers, lodging — whose viability depends on decisions made in offices far from the trailhead.

What we cover

Access and land policy. Permits, quotas, closures and the fee structures that determine who can operate where.

The recreation economy. Guiding and outfitting, gear retail, and the rural economies where outdoor visitation is a primary employer.

Conservation funding. Where it comes from, what it actually pays for, and the recurring tension between use and preservation.

Regional coverage. With a working bias toward the Mountain West, where our own knowledge is first-hand rather than researched.

Standing beats

  • Guiding and outfitting economics
