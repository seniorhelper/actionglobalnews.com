Health coverage tends to split into two unhelpful halves: breathless research headlines on one side and dense clinical guidance on the other. This desk sits in between. We focus on the health decisions families and small organizations actually make, such as how to adapt a home for an aging parent, what the evidence says about popular medications, and how to judge a telehealth program, and we cite the primary sources behind every figure.

Two themes run through our coverage. The first is aging in place. The number of older Americans is growing, falls are the leading cause of injury for adults 65 and older, and the decisions that let someone stay at home safely are often small, practical and made in a hurry. The second is the rapid spread of GLP-1 medications, which have changed weight management and raised new questions about nutrition, muscle, cost and access.

How we handle health information

Primary sources. We cite the CDC, peer-reviewed research and professional society guidance directly, and we say when evidence is thin.

No medical advice. Our reporting explains what research shows. Individual decisions belong with a licensed clinician.

Clear disclosure. Some health businesses we write about are clients of, or share ownership with, the publisher. Those articles are labeled at the top, and the relationship is stated plainly.

Frequently asked

Is the health information on Action Global News medical advice?

No. Our reporting summarizes research and official guidance with sources. Decisions about treatment should be made with a licensed clinician.

Why do some health articles carry a Sponsored or From our network label?

Articles about businesses that share ownership with the publisher are labeled From our network; articles about clients of its marketing agency are labeled Sponsored. The relationship is explained at the top.

Where do your falls and aging statistics come from?

Primarily from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the National Safety Council, cited and linked in each article.
