Business & Markets

Markets, macro and the operating reality underneath the headline numbers — with an eye on what it means for companies without a treasury department.

Most business journalism is written for people who own shares in companies. This desk is written for people who own companies — a meaningfully different audience with meaningfully different questions.

A quarterly earnings miss at a listed multinational is interesting if you hold the stock. If you run a twelve-person firm, the useful version of that story is what it signals about the conditions you are about to operate in: whether credit is tightening, whether your customers are deferring purchases, whether the input costs you cannot control are moving.

How we approach it

Macro, translated. We cover rates, inflation and credit conditions in terms of their consequences for operating businesses rather than for asset allocation.

The operating layer. Pricing, margin, cash conversion, hiring. The things that determine whether a business survives a difficult year, which are rarely the things that determine a headline.

Sector specificity. Home services, trades, professional services and small-to-mid ecommerce get more attention here than their share of market capitalisation would justify, because they get less attention elsewhere than their share of employment would justify.
