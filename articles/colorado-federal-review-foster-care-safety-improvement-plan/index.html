Colorado · Brief

A federal review found Colorado fell short on six of seven child welfare outcomes, and an improvement plan is due in December

The Children's Bureau rated Colorado statistically worse than national performance on two key safety measures. State officials say they welcome the review, and the state's improvement plan goes to federal officials next month.

AGN Action Global News Staff Edited by Zach Wennstedt · Published November 18, 2026 · 2 min read
LinkedInXEmail

Key takeaways

  • Colorado met federal standards on one of seven child welfare outcomes in the 2026 review.
  • El Paso and Denver counties together accounted for 48.8% of the state's repeat maltreatment victims.
  • Colorado must submit a performance improvement plan to federal officials in December 2026.

Colorado's child welfare system fell short of federal standards on six of seven outcomes in a review by the U.S. Children's Bureau, and the state must answer with an improvement plan in December. The final report rests on a statewide assessment, federal data and case reviews conducted July 20 to 24 in Denver, Mesa County and Weld County. The Colorado Sun reported the findings and the December deadline on Wednesday.

What did reviewers find?

The state was statistically worse than national performance on two safety indicators: maltreatment of children in foster care and repeat maltreatment within 12 months. The report says Colorado's observed rate of victimization in care rose from 7.56 per 100,000 days in fiscal 2021 to 8.84 in fiscal 2023, as days in care fell by almost 14% while the number of victimizations held about steady. The Sun, citing the review, put the rate at about 12 per 100,000 days.

El Paso and Denver counties together accounted for 48.8% of repeat victims. Denver has improved, cutting its recurrence rate from 15.4% in fiscal 2021-22 to 11.3% in fiscal 2023-24, the report says.

Reviewers rated 85% of cases as meeting the standard for protecting children from abuse and neglect, short of the 95% required. Where cases fell short, the report says, agencies did not respond to maltreatment reports within the time set by state policy. Stability and permanency for children in foster care was the weakest area, at 35% of cases.

The report also recorded strengths. Colorado met the standard on children's educational needs and on three of seven systemic factors, and reviewers found no unaddressed safety concerns for the children in the foster homes and facilities they examined.

What happens next

The state's draft plan emphasizes support for families whose child welfare cases have closed, and Colorado is joining a federal foster home recruitment pilot called A Home for Every Child, the Sun reported. In an emailed response to the newspaper, the Colorado Department of Human Services said, "We welcome these reviews as an opportunity to continually improve our child welfare system."
