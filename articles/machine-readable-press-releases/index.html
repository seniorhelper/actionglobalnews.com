A press release written for the machines that now decide who gets quoted

Traditional wires sell reach to newsrooms. A newer kind of wire sells something narrower and, for small companies, often more useful: a permanent, dated, attributed record that search engines and AI systems can read without guessing.

AGN Action Global News Technology desk · Published October 6, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Business Wire lists pay-per-release distribution starting at $475 for 400 words with U.S. local distribution.
  • PressReleaseForBusiness.com publishes releases at permanent addresses with explicit dates, named spokespeople and structured markup, with published tiers starting at $19.99.
  • The value is a citable, dated record about a business, not links: releases are permanently labeled as announcements and do not sell followed links.
  • A release only helps if it contains real news written as checkable facts, with the opening sentences complete on their own.

The press release has had a strange decade. As a link-building trick, it died years ago, when search engines learned to discount links in syndicated announcements and made clear they expected those links to be marked as such. As a format, it has quietly become more useful than ever. A dated, attributed document that states who announced what, where and when is exactly the kind of source that search engines and AI answer systems are comfortable citing. We made that argument at length in our earlier analysis of the press release’s second life.

What has been missing is a publishing option built for that use, at a price a small business can justify for routine news. Traditional wire services are built around reach to newsrooms and priced accordingly: Business Wire, for instance, lists pay-per-release distribution starting at $475 for 400 words with U.S. local distribution, with costs rising for broader circuits, more words and multimedia.

A different product, not a cheaper copy

PressReleaseForBusiness.com, which describes itself as the newswire for machine-readable announcements, takes the second path. Rather than promising pickup by hundreds of outlets, it focuses on the properties that make a document citable. Every release gets a permanent address, an explicit publication date, a named spokesperson and structured markup that tells a retrieval system exactly what it is looking at. Releases are permanently labeled as announcements, and the service does not sell followed links, which keeps it on the right side of search engine guidelines.

Its published tiers start at $19.99 for a standard release of up to 1,400 words, with an optimized tier that adds an editorial pass to tighten facts and structure, and a network tier that places the release across a relevance-matched set of related properties. Client-requested revisions after a draft is approved carry a small fee, and the service describes network placement as eligible rather than guaranteed. For a small business, that changes the math: publishing a dated record of every real milestone becomes a routine marketing task rather than a once-a-year budget decision. Companies that want to try it can start with AI-ready press release distribution at PressReleaseForBusiness.com.

The value is not the link. It is a permanent, dated, attributed record that a machine can quote without guessing.

What makes a release usable by machines

  1. An opening that stands alone. The first forty words should state who, what, where and when, and be true and complete if quoted in isolation.
  2. Facts instead of adjectives. “Leading provider” cannot be cited. “Opened a second location in Aurora, Colorado, on October 1” can.
  3. A named person. A quotation attributed to a named spokesperson with a title is more citable than a statement from “the company.”
  4. Explicit dates and geography. When it happened, when it takes effect, and exactly where.
  5. Structured markup. Document type, publisher, dates and the entities involved, stated rather than inferred.
  6. One canonical address. A single permanent URL, with any syndicated copies pointing back to it.

What a release will not do

Honesty about limits is part of the pitch. A structured release does not buy rankings, does not guarantee media coverage, and will not rescue an announcement with no news in it. It is a record, not a campaign. Businesses that publish genuine milestones, such as openings, hires, launches, certifications, partnerships and results, over months build a trail of dated, consistent facts about themselves. That trail is what answer engines draw on when someone asks who does what in a given place.

Frequently asked

How much does press release distribution cost?

Traditional wires such as Business Wire list pay-per-release distribution starting at $475 for 400 words with U.S. local distribution. Structured newswires such as PressReleaseForBusiness.com publish tiers starting at $19.99, but do not include traditional newsroom circuits.

Do press releases still help SEO?

Not through links; search engines discount links in syndicated releases. A release can help by creating a dated, attributed, factual record about a business that search engines and AI systems can find and cite.

What is a machine-readable press release?

It is a release published at a permanent URL with an explicit date, a named spokesperson, concrete verifiable facts and structured markup that states the document type, publisher and entities involved.

Should links in press releases be nofollow?

Links in paid or syndicated announcements should carry the attributes search engines expect for sponsored content. Treating releases as a link-building tool is the pattern search engines act against.
