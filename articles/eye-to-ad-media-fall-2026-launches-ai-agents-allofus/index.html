Eye To Ad Media's fall launches: an AI agent factory, a member-controlled social network and a press release network. What each does, what it costs and who it suits

The Denver marketing firm, which shares ownership with Action Global News, is rolling out several products this fall. This overview sets out what each one does, the published prices, who it is meant for and what a small business owner should check before signing up.

Eye To Ad Media, a Denver marketing and web development firm founded in 2012, is launching several products this fall aimed at small and mid-sized businesses and at people looking for a different kind of social network. The company is based at 1001 Bannock St., Suite 660, in Denver and can be reached at 1-800-481-8638. As the disclosure above explains, it shares ownership with Action Global News, so this article sticks to what the products do, what the company publishes about price, and the questions a buyer should ask.

My AI Agent Army: AI agents built to order

My AI Agent Army describes itself as an "AI agent factory." An AI agent here is a piece of software assigned one recurring job, such as replying to new leads, chasing overdue invoices or drafting social posts, connected to the tools a business already uses. Customers can order one agent or hundreds.

There are two ways to buy. Ready-made agents start from the company's templates and are set up with a business's details, rules and tools. Custom-built agents are coded for workflows that a template does not cover, and the custom code written for a client belongs to that client. In both cases the client owns its agent setup and its data.

Projects begin with an Agent Blueprint planning session that costs $750 and is credited toward the build. It maps where a business's hours go and which agents to build first.

The 17 agents already running

Seventeen agents are built and tested, and the company has published their real output on a sample company, a fictional plumbing business with a sample inbox, calendar, invoices and bank exports, on its See Them Work page:

  • Operations: Daily Briefing, Meeting to Actions, Desktop Organizer and Commander, which coordinates the other agents.
  • Sales and service: Speed-to-Lead Responder, Review Request and Customer Reactivation.
  • Admin and finance: Invoice Reminder, P&L Builder, Spend Leak Finder and Renewal Sentinel, which tracks domains, SSL certificates, licenses and contracts.
  • Marketing: a Google Business Profile poster, Content Repurposer, AI Visibility, which tracks whether AI assistants recommend a business, and Listing Consistency.
  • Web and AI readiness: Site Health and Agent-Ready Auditor, which scores how ready a business is for AI assistants and agents that shop and book on customers' behalf, and writes fixes such as an llms.txt file and structured data markup.

The company says every agent drafts its work and waits for a person to approve it; nothing is sent automatically. Every agent works both without an AI model and with one, and the suite has more than 300 automated tests.

Who it suits: owners and small teams who lose hours each week to repeatable tasks such as follow-ups, reminders, reports and listings, and who are willing to review drafts rather than hand everything to software.

What to check first:

  • Total cost over time. Compare owning an agent with Subscribe-to-Own over two to four years, the way you would compare buying and leasing equipment.
  • Integration scope. The pricing page says building an agent is priced up front, while connecting it to a business's phone system, CRM, inbox and payment tools is scoped separately after the Blueprint. Get that scope in writing.
  • Approval workload. Human approval is the safety net, so plan who will review drafts and how quickly.
  • Data quality. An invoice or bookkeeping agent can only be as accurate as the records it reads.

For a broader look at when a free AI agent is enough and when a custom one is worth it, see our explainer on custom AI agents for small businesses.

allofus.one: a free social platform you control

allofus.one is a free social platform built in Denver and in public beta since October 11, 2026. One account works three ways: a chronological feed, a 3D world that runs in a phone or laptop browser, and the same world in virtual reality on a Meta Quest headset through WebXR, with nothing to install.

The pitch is control. The feed shows posts in the order they were made, with no ranking algorithm deciding what appears first. Members can switch sponsored posts off in their ad settings, download their data, or delete their account at any time.

Built-in tools are free: the LIFEboard planner for goals, habits, journaling, a focus timer and budgeting; Groups and Spaces; Events with free video rooms; Courses; direct messages; video calls for up to six people; and Brand pages that let a small business run a page from a personal profile. Accounts are open to anyone 13 and older, and the platform has a language filter plus report and block tools.

Who it suits: people who want a feed of the friends and groups they chose, and community organizers, coaches, clubs and small businesses that need events, courses and video calls without paying for separate software.

What to check first: it is a beta, so expect rough edges and report bugs. A new network also starts small; a business page is most useful if its customers are willing to join. The VR mode requires a Quest headset, though the feed and the 3D world work in an ordinary browser.

A press release network that labels everything

Distribute Press Releases and Press Release For Business form the company's press release network. Each release is routed to sites whose topic and location match it, rather than blasted to a fixed list, and every release is labeled as a press release wherever it appears. A single release on Distribute Press Releases is listed at $99.

Readers of this site should know how that touches Action Global News. Releases routed by Distribute Press Releases are republished in our announcements section, each labeled as a company statement, with a link to the original as the canonical source. They are not Action Global News reporting. Our earlier analysis of press releases written for search engines and AI systems explains what this kind of release is good for and where it falls short.

What to check first: a release is useful when it contains real, checkable news. No release service, including this one, can promise coverage by outlets it does not control.

Also from Eye To Ad Media

Two earlier launches round out the lineup. USABusinessSearch.com is a free national business directory built on open map data, covered in our article on the free business directory. And the company's subscribe-to-own website program, in which a business pays monthly for a custom site with a path to ownership, is examined in rent, buy or subscribe to own, which also covers the questions to ask about any monthly website plan.

The useful question for any of these products is the same one to ask of any vendor: what exactly do I own, what will it cost over three years, and who checks the work?

Frequently asked

How much does a My AI Agent Army agent cost?

Ready-made agents can be owned from $1,500 (Scout), $3,500 (Specialist) or $7,500 (Commander), or subscribed to from $149, $299 or $599 per agent per month plus a one-time setup of $500, $1,000 or $1,500. Custom builds start at $4,000. Published prices are starting points.

Is allofus.one free?

Yes. allofus.one is a free social platform in public beta since October 11, 2026. Sponsored posts are labeled, and members can switch them off in their ad settings.
