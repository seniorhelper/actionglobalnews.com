AAA expects 81.5 million Americans to travel for Thanksgiving, slightly fewer than last year as gas and airfare cost more

The forecast ends three straight years of record Thanksgiving travel. Driving and flying are down a little, while buses, trains and cruises are up.

AGN Action Global News Staff Edited by Zach Wennstedt · Published November 24, 2026 · 2 min read
LinkedInXEmail

Key takeaways

  • AAA projects 81.5 million travelers from November 24 to 30, 2026, about 121,000 fewer than last year.
  • Round-trip domestic flights booked through AAA average $800, up 17% from last year.
  • Regular gas averaged $3.63 a gallon on November 23, 56 cents more than a year earlier, NPR reported.

About 81.5 million Americans are expected to travel at least 50 miles from home between Tuesday, November 24, and Monday, November 30, according to AAA's Thanksgiving forecast, released on November 16, 2026. That is about 121,000 fewer people than the 81.6 million who traveled last year, a decline of 0.1%. It ends a three-year run of record Thanksgiving travel that began in 2023.

How are people traveling?

Nearly 90% will drive. AAA projects 72.9 million people will travel by car, down from 73.1 million last year. Another 5.96 million are expected to take domestic flights, a 0.2% decrease. Buses, trains and cruises are the growing category, with 2.6 million travelers, up 4.1%.

AAA said higher travel costs are part of the reason for the dip, but it expects roads and airports to feel about as busy as last year.

What is it costing?

A gallon of regular gas averaged $3.63 on Monday, November 23, which was 56 cents more than a year earlier, NPR reported. The highest Thanksgiving Day average on record was $3.58 in 2022, according to AAA. NPR tied this year's prices to tighter crude oil supply through the Strait of Hormuz since the U.S. war in Iran began in February.

Flights cost more too, though estimates differ. AAA says round-trip domestic tickets booked through its service are 17% more expensive, averaging $800. The travel company Hopper told NPR that Thanksgiving airfares are up about 30%. Hopper's lead economist, Hayley Berg, said jet fuel costs almost 90% more than a year ago.

How busy will airports and buses be?

The Transportation Security Administration expects to screen 17.6 million passengers from Tuesday through next Monday, NPR reported, with more than 3 million possible on Sunday alone. Flix North America, which owns Greyhound, told NPR its bookings for the week are up 17% from last year.

What happens next

Gas prices have been falling in recent weeks after staying above $4 for much of the year, NPR reported. Analysts told NPR that prices remain volatile and could rise again if the war escalates. Many people have already booked December trips at this fall's prices.
