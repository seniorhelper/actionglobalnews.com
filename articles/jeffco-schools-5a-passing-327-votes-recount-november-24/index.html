Jeffco Public Schools' $73 million tax measure 5A has flipped to passing by 327 votes and now goes to a recount

The teacher pay measure trailed for more than a week after Election Day before late ballots pushed it ahead. The margin is small enough that Colorado law requires a recount before the result is final.

AGN Action Global News Staff Edited by Zach Wennstedt · Published November 15, 2026 · 2 min read
LinkedInXEmail

Key takeaways

  • Ballot Issue 5A, a $73 million tax increase, was passing by 327 votes in the final tally reported November 13, 2026.
  • The recount starts November 24 and must be finished by December 4.
  • Ballot Issue 5B, $60 million for building maintenance, failed by 7,302 votes, 51% to 49%.

A $73 million property tax measure for Jeffco Public Schools that looked headed for defeat is now passing. Ballot Issue 5A was ahead by 327 votes in the final tally, Chalkbeat Colorado reported on Friday, November 13. The margin is narrow enough to require a recount.

The companion measure did not recover. Ballot Issue 5B, which would have raised $60 million for building maintenance, failed by 7,302 votes, 51% to 49%.

How did 5A flip?

Early counts showed 5A losing, and at one point the gap was as small as three votes. When we last reported on the race on November 9, it trailed by 377. The result changed after November 12, the deadline for military and overseas ballots to arrive and for voters to fix problems such as signature errors, according to Chalkbeat.

How will the recount work?

Colorado law calls for a recount when the margin is one half of one percent or less. This one starts November 24 and must be done by December 4. Election officials will rescan every ballot, and bipartisan teams will review marks that are unclear, Chalkbeat reported. Jefferson County Clerk and Recorder Amanda Gonzalez will oversee it, a Colorado Sun opinion column noted.

What does 5A pay for?

Most of the money would cover pay raises, both already promised and future, for teachers, principals and support staff. It would also expand career and technical education. About $8.5 million would go to charter schools, as state law requires. Jeffco is Colorado's second-largest district, with 74,000 students, and its leaders have pointed to falling enrollment as a source of budget pressure.

What happens next

The stakes for next year's budget are large. Interim Superintendent Rob Stein has said the district would face at least $13 million in budget reductions if both measures failed, Denver7 reported before the final tally. The outcome will not be official until the recount is complete.
