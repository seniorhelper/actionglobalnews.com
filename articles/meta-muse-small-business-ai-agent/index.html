Meta just handed small businesses a free AI agent. Here is what Muse can touch, and what it cannot

Muse for Small Business connects to the tools owners already use and drafts the plans, campaigns and emails they never have time for. The free tier is the headline. The permissions are the story.

AGN Action Global News AI Agents desk · Published October 8, 2026 · 6 min read
LinkedInXFacebookEmail

Key takeaways

  • Meta launched Muse for Small Business on September 29, 2026, a business version of its Muse personal agent.
  • It connects to tools including Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Canva, Notion, Slack, Dropbox and Zoom, plus Instagram analytics, Facebook Pages and Meta ad accounts.
  • Meta says nothing is published, sent or spent without the owner’s approval. It is available in the U.S. and Canada to users 18 and over.
  • Pricing follows the consumer Muse app: free with usage limits, with paid tiers for heavier use. Meta did not publish separate business pricing.

Meta has launched Muse for Small Business, a version of its Muse AI agent built for owners and their teams. Announced on September 29, the product arrived a day after the company unveiled a broader enterprise push and named former MongoDB chief executive Chirantan “CJ” Desai to lead it.

Where most business AI tools answer questions, Muse is designed to work toward goals. Meta’s own example is a growth plan the agent builds after analyzing a company’s sales and social media data alongside its stated objectives. Meta says it can also help manage cash flow and inventory, draft customer emails, and create ads based on what has worked before.

What it connects to

The connections are what make it an agent rather than a chatbot. Muse for Small Business links to a company’s Instagram analytics, Facebook Pages and Meta ad accounts, and to outside tools including Shopify, Stripe, QuickBooks, Canva, Notion, Slack, Dropbox, Figma, Asana and Zoom.

That reach is the point, and also the reason to slow down. An agent that can read a Shopify store, a Stripe account and a QuickBooks ledger can see most of a small business. Shopify, for example, describes connected tools as reading store data and changing it only if the owner grants write access. Meta has not said which level of access Muse asks for first, so owners should check each connection screen rather than clicking through.

The free tier is the headline. The permissions screen is where the real decision gets made.

Approvals, availability and price

Meta says the agent checks with the owner before it publishes, sends, spends or shares anything. That approval step is the single most important safeguard in any agent that can act on a business’s behalf, and it is consistent with what regulators are now signaling about who answers for an agent’s actions.

The product is available in the United States and Canada to people 18 and over. Meta has not given a timeline for other regions, and its subscription help pages describe paid Muse plans as still in limited testing.

On price, CNBC reported that the business version follows the existing Muse app, which is free with usage limits and offers subscriptions beyond that. Several outlets have reported paid tiers of $20 and $100 a month, metered by usage, and reporting describes subscriptions as tied to an individual Meta account rather than pooled across a team. Meta’s AI products vice president Vishal Shah told Axios that businesses using more of the service may run into limits.

Questions to ask before you connect anything

  • Which accounts does it need, and at what level? Start with read-only connections and add write access only where a task requires it.
  • Who on the team can approve actions? If several people share a login, decide who signs off on ads, emails and spending.
  • What happens to your data? Read the data-use terms for each connection, especially customer and payment records.
  • What is the plan when it is wrong? Draft campaigns and emails still need a human read. Treat the agent as a fast first draft, not a final answer.
  • Does a general agent fit your process? Off-the-shelf agents are built for common tasks. Businesses with unusual workflows, regulated data or systems Meta does not connect to may need something built to fit.

Why it matters

Muse for Small Business is the clearest sign yet that the largest platforms see agents, not chatbots, as the next battleground for small business software. A free agent from a company that already runs many small firms’ advertising lowers the barrier to trying one. It also concentrates a great deal of a business’s operating data inside one platform. Owners who go in with tight permissions and a clear approval process will get the time savings without handing over more control than they meant to.
