Tuberculosis deaths have fallen 35% since 2015, the WHO says, but donor funding dropped 14% last year

The world has hit its first target for cutting tuberculosis deaths, five years late. Governments spent more of their own money in 2025 as international aid shrank.

AGN Action Global News Staff Edited by Zach Wennstedt · Published November 13, 2026 · 2 min read
LinkedInXEmail

Key takeaways

  • An estimated 10.6 million people developed tuberculosis in 2025 and about 1.1 million died, the WHO said November 12.
  • Domestic TB funding rose 14% to $5.6 billion while donor funding fell 14% to $0.94 billion; the 2027 target is $22 billion a year.
  • About 2.2 million people with TB were not diagnosed or reported in 2025.

An estimated 10.6 million people fell ill with tuberculosis in 2025 and 1.1 million died, the World Health Organization said in its Global tuberculosis report 2026, published Thursday, November 12. Deaths are down 35% since 2015. That meets the first milestone of the agency's End TB Strategy five years later than planned, according to the WHO's news release.

The rate of new cases has fallen 14% since 2015, well short of the 50% target set for 2025. Tuberculosis remains the leading cause of death from a single infectious agent, the WHO said. The report draws on data from 187 countries and areas.

What is happening with funding?

Governments raised their own spending on tuberculosis by 14% between 2024 and 2025, to $5.6 billion, partly making up for a drop of similar size in international money, the WHO said. Donor funding fell 14%, from $1.1 billion to $0.94 billion, CIDRAP reported. Total funding of $6.6 billion is far below the $22 billion a year that United Nations member states set as a goal for 2027.

"Many governments have dug deeper into their own budgets to protect the gains they have made," WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said in the release.

Where are the gaps?

Of the 10.6 million people who developed the disease, 8.4 million were diagnosed, leaving about 2.2 million undiagnosed or unreported. Thirty countries accounted for 88% of cases. In the Americas, the rate of new cases rose for a fifth straight year and is 13% above its 2015 level.

About 370,000 people developed tuberculosis resistant to the drug rifampicin, and 42% of them received treatment. Among those treated, use of a six-month regimen recommended by the WHO rose from 18% to 42% in a year.

What happens next

Sixteen vaccine candidates are in development, CIDRAP reported. A late-stage trial of one, called M72, is expected to show by 2028 whether it protects older children and adults. The strategy's 2035 goal is a 90% cut in deaths from 2015 levels.
